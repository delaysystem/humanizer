The accuracy of the state's official broadband coverage map is under intense debate, leaving millions of dollars in federal funding in limbo. The map, compiled from data self-reported by internet service providers, suggests that most addresses have high-speed service. However, a coalition of rural counties disputes these figures, presenting only ten thousand speed tests, indicating that the coverage has been significantly exaggerated. The main reason for this discrepancy is the mapping method; under it, even if there is service at one location in an entire census block, it is classified as having service. While critics and providers agree that this method is flawed, there is still strong disagreement about the true extent of coverage. The outcome is crucial because federal infrastructure grants are distributed based on the map's findings. Communities incorrectly identified could lose funding entirely. Providers argue that the data collected by volunteers is unreliable and cite their own measurements showing sufficient service. Meanwhile, residents in disputed areas describe daily struggles with unreliable connections, undermining remote work and education. The legislature mandated a new mapping process two years ago, but implementation has been hampered by delays and changes among contractors. A court hearing scheduled for January could reshape the financial landscape. Federal officials warn that grants could be re-examined if the original data is proven to be factually incorrect. Technical experts recommend address-level mapping as the only reliable way forward, although providers argue that the cost is prohibitive. Several states have launched independent verification programs using their own testing infrastructure. These efforts face statistical debates regarding representative sampling and measurement standards. Consumer advocacy groups continue to collect crowdsourced data to supplement official statistics. This controversy has drawn national attention as other states face similar discrepancies in their own coverage claims.
