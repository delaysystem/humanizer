The Riverside School District is reconsidering a plan to purchase thousands of new laptops for students after bids exceeded the allocated budget. The district had intended to replace its older laptops with a standard model featuring better battery life and durability. However, rising unit costs and new tariffs caused even a lower qualifying bid to significantly exceed the budget. Board members are now considering several alternatives, including splitting purchases among multiple vendors, delaying the upgrade, or securing additional funds through voting. Teachers have expressed strong support for moving forward without delay, citing the poor condition of current devices. The technology department warns that managing multiple models would strain limited support resources. A final decision is expected at next month's board meeting. Parent organizations have rallied behind the upgrade, arguing that older devices are inconvenient for students in an increasingly digital curriculum. District grant writers are seeking federal connections funds that could offset part of the shortfall. Neighboring districts have reported similar purchasing difficulties, suggesting systemic market pressures rather than local mismanagement. Trade-in values for older machines might recover a small portion of the needed funds.
