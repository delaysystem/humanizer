Vaccines provide protection against diseases by training the immune system to recognize threats before a true infection occurs. A vaccine contains harmless pathogens, such as a weakened virus or part of it. When administered, the immune system responds by producing antibodies and forming memory cells. These memory cells persist for years, allowing for a quick response if the actual pathogen appears. As a result, vaccinated individuals can fight off infections before symptoms develop. Booster doses help maintain this protection as natural immunity wanes over time.
