Tides on Earth are primarily caused by the gravitational pull of the Moon on water. The Moon's gravity pulls water towards it, creating a bulge on its surface. The Earth's rotation creates a bulge in the opposite direction due to centrifugal force. As the planet rotates through these bulges, most coastal areas experience high and low tides twice a day. The Sun's gravity also plays a role. Tides are particularly significant when the Sun and Moon are aligned, but weaker tides occur at perpendicular positions.
