Vaccines protect against diseases by training the immune system to recognize threats before a real infection occurs. A vaccine contains harmless forms of pathogens, such as a weakened virus or part of one. When given, the immune system responds by producing antibodies and forming memory cells. These memory cells persist for years, allowing for a quick response if the actual pathogen appears. As a result, vaccinated individuals can fight off infections before symptoms develop. Booster doses help maintain this protection when immunity naturally wanes over time.
