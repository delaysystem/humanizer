The tides on Earth are primarily caused by the gravitational pull of the Moon on the water's surface. The Moon's gravity pulls water towards it, creating a bulge in its surface. The Earth's rotation creates a counter-bulge in the opposite direction due to centrifugal forces. As the planet rotates through these bulges, most coastal areas experience high tides twice a day and low tides twice a day. The Sun's gravity also plays a role. When the Sun and Moon align, the tides are particularly significant, while perpendicular positions produce weaker tides.
