Tides are primarily caused by the gravitational pull of the moon on the Earth's water. The moon's gravity pulls water towards it, creating a bulge on its surface. The Earth's rotation creates a counter-bulge in the opposite direction due to centrifugal forces. As the planet rotates through these bulges, most coastal areas experience high tides twice and low tides twice each day. The sun's gravity also plays a role. When the sun and moon align, tides are particularly significant, while perpendicular positions produce weaker tides.
