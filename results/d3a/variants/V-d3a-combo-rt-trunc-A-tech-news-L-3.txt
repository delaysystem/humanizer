Labor negotiations at one of the country's busiest container ports have reached a critical stage, with a union agreement now just days away from being finalized. The port authority's plan to expand automation to additional terminals is at the center of the dispute. The administration argues that automated cranes will dramatically improve efficiency, citing significantly faster ship turnaround times at an existing automated berth. Union leaders contend that while cargo volumes are reaching new records, automation has consistently reduced membership. They question whether productivity statistics take into account system downtime and the full cost of maintenance. This issue isn't limited to the waterway alone.
