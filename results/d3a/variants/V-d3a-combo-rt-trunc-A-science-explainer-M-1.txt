Scientists are conducting open-field trials of wheat modified with CRISPR gene-editing technology, marking a significant milestone for agricultural biotechnology. Unlike traditional genetic modifications that introduce foreign DNA, these changes involve precise deletions within the plant's own genome. This distinction has important regulatory implications, as many authorities classify such changes alongside conventional breeding methods. Initial results suggest that edited wheat significantly better resists fungal disease than conventional varieties, without affecting yield. Researchers emphasize that confirming these findings will require several growing seasons. This technology raises broader questions about the density of agricultural patents and the management of gene flow to wild relatives.
