The annual influenza vaccine remains one of public health's persistent challenges. Scientists must predict which viral strains will circulate months in advance. Production constraints force decisions about strain selection as early as February for the upcoming winter, and mismatches can significantly reduce effectiveness over several years. Messenger RNA (mRNA) vaccine platforms offer potential solutions to these limitations. mRNA vaccines can be designed and manufactured much faster than traditional egg-based products. Therefore, strain selection may occur closer to the flu season when predictions are more reliable. Additionally, mRNA technology allows for the inclusion of multiple strain components or less variable conserved viral elements between seasons.
