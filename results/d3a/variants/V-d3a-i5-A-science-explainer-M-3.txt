Researchers have discovered microplastic particles in rain around the world, from major cities to remote polar regions. These tiny fragments originate from sources including synthetic textiles, packaging materials, and vehicle tires. The wind carries them far, and then rain collects them. A recent multi-city study found measurable concentrations in every sample collected, with higher levels near industrial areas. Health effects are unclear. Most animal studies suggest they may cause tissue inflammation, but real-world assessments involve rare and diverse particles. Scientists have identified two urgent needs: standardized measurement methods that allow for comparison of studies, and a good source characterization to guide policy responses. Until these gaps are addressed, experts recommend continuous monitoring without alarm. Ocean samples reveal similar ubiquity, with particles detected from surface waters to deep-sea sediments. Freshwater systems show comparable contamination, raising questions about the effectiveness of drinking water treatment. Researchers are developing screening criteria to help utilities assess their removal rates. International coordination is increasing as several nations align monitoring protocols to enable global comparisons. Policy responses range from single-use plastic bans to washing machine filter orders.
