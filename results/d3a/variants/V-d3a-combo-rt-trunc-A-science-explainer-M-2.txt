Earthquake early warning systems save lives by using different speeds of seismic waves. When an earthquake occurs, it generates fast-moving P-waves, followed by slower but more destructive S-waves. Sensors near the epicenter detect the initial waves, and automated systems quickly estimate the magnitude of the earthquake. Warnings are sent to areas where damaging waves have not yet arrived, providing a one-second to one-minute lead time. This short time allows for automatic protective measures such as slowing down trains and opening elevator doors. Japan operates the world's most advanced system, while California has deployed statewide coverage in recent years.
