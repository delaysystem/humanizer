Fecal microbiota transplantation, which involves transferring gut microbes from healthy donors to patients, has emerged as a remarkably effective treatment for recurring intestinal infections. For patients suffering from Clostridioides difficile infection, which is resistant to common antibiotics and causes thousands of deaths annually, transplantation procedures achieve cure rates of up to ninety percent. Regulatory agencies have now approved standard commercial products, bringing stability to a field that began with informal donor arrangements. Building on this success, researchers are exploring applications for a wide range of conditions associated with gut microbial imbalance.
