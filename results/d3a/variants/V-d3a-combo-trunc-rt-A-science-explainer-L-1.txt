The annual influenza vaccine is one of public health's ongoing challenges. Scientists must predict which viral strains will circulate months in advance. Production constraints force strain selection decisions as early as February for the upcoming winter season, and a mismatch can significantly reduce effectiveness over several years. Messenger RNA vaccine platforms offer potential solutions to these limitations. mRNA vaccines can be designed and manufactured much faster than traditional egg-based products. Therefore, strain selection may occur closer to the flu season when predictions are more reliable. Additionally, mRNA technology allows for the inclusion of conserved viral elements that change less between multiple strain components or seasons. Recent clinical trials have shown encouraging results, particularly with improved immune responses to traditional vaccines in older adults.
