The accuracy of the state's official broadband coverage map remains a subject of intense debate, leaving hundreds of millions of dollars in federal funding in limbo. The map, compiled from self-reported data by internet service providers, suggests that most addresses have high-speed service. However, a coalition of rural counties disputes these figures, presenting speed tests obtained from tens of thousands of people, indicating that the coverage is significantly exaggerated. The primary reason for this discrepancy lies in the mapping method; under it, if even one location within an entire census block has service, it is classified as having service.
