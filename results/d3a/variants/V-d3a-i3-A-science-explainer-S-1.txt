The reason sunsets appear red is due to the way Earth's atmosphere scatters sunlight. When the sun is high in the sky, light travels a relatively short distance through the air and blue wavelengths are scattered in all directions, making the sky appear blue. Closer to sunset, sunlight has to travel through more of the atmosphere to reach your eyes. During this longer journey, most of the blue light is scattered, leaving primarily red and orange wavelengths. Particles such as dust and pollution can intensify these colors. That's why sunsets are often more vibrant after thunderstorms or volcanic activity.
