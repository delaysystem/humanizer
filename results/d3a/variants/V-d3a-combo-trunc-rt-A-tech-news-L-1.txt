The state's official broadband coverage map is the subject of intense debate over its accuracy, and the fate of billions of dollars in federal funding hangs in the balance. The map, compiled from self-reported data by internet service providers, suggests that a large majority of addresses have high-speed service. However, a coalition of rural counties disputes these figures, presenting speed tests from tens of thousands of people that indicate significantly overestimated coverage. The primary reason for this discrepancy lies in the mapping methodology, which classifies an entire census block as served even if only one location within it has service. While critics and providers agree that this method is flawed, they remain divided.
