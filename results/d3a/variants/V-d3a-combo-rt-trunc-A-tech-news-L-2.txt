The massive semiconductor manufacturing facility under construction outside Columbus faces a critical non-technical challenge: finding enough qualified workers. When fully operational, this facility is expected to employ thousands of technicians, engineers, and support staff. However, the specialized skills required for chipmaking are scarce throughout the region. Local community colleges have responded by quickly developing new training programs, including clean-room laboratories and accelerated degree tracks. Enrollments have exceeded expectations, showing strong interest among future students. Yet, experienced professionals earn higher salaries in industry than in academia, so programs face their own staffing shortages.
