In late 2022, researchers at the National Laboratory of California achieved a historic milestone in fusion energy. Lasers produced more energy from the fusion reaction than they delivered to the fuel. This achievement, known as scientific ignition, represents the culmination of decades of research effort. The result requires significant background: while the fusion reaction released more energy than the laser light that struck the target, the lasers consumed a lot of electricity from the grid. The actual energy gain, measured by wall-plug power, remains a distant goal. However, this demonstration proved that laboratory ignition is physically achievable, and subsequent experiments have improved the initial result. The journey from scientific demonstration to practical power generation involves major challenges.
