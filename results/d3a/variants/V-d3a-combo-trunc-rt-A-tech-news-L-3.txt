Labor negotiations at one of the country's busiest container ports have reached a critical point, with a union contract set to expire in just a few days. The port authority's plan to expand automation to additional terminals is at the center of the dispute. The administration argues that automated cranes will dramatically improve efficiency, citing significantly faster ship turnaround times at an existing automated berth. However, union leaders counter that automation has consistently eroded membership. They question whether productivity statistics take into account system downtime and the full cost of maintenance. This issue isn't limited to water resources; economists warn that even a minor work stoppage can disrupt supply chains.
