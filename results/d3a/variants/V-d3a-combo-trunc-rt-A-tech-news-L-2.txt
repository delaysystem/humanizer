The massive semiconductor manufacturing facility under construction outside of Columbus is facing a critical challenge that has nothing to do with technology: finding enough qualified workers. The facility is expected to employ thousands of technicians, engineers and support staff when it is fully operational. However, the specialized skills required for chipmaking are scarce throughout the region. Local community colleges have responded by quickly developing new training programs, including clean-room labs and accelerated degree courses. Enrollments have exceeded expectations, showing strong interest among future students. Nevertheless, these programs face their own staffing shortages as experienced professionals tend to earn higher salaries in industry than in academia. Housing availability is another hurdle, with rental prices rising sharply.
