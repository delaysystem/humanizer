Earthquake early warning systems save lives by using different speeds of seismic waves. When an earthquake occurs, it generates fast-moving P-waves followed by slower but more destructive S-waves. Sensors near the epicenter detect the initial waves, and automated systems quickly estimate the magnitude of the earthquake. Warnings are sent to areas where damaging waves have not yet reached, providing a lead time of several seconds to a minute. This short window allows for automatic protective measures such as slowing down trains and opening elevator doors. Japan operates one of the world's most advanced systems, while California has implemented statewide coverage in recent years.
