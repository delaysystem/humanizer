Scientists are conducting open field trials of wheat modified with CRISPR gene-editing technology, marking a significant milestone for agricultural biotechnology. Unlike traditional genetic modification, which introduces foreign DNA, these edits involve precise deletions within the plant's own genome. This distinction has important regulatory implications, as many authorities classify such edits alongside conventional breeding methods. Initial results suggest the edited wheat resists fungal disease substantially better than conventional varieties, without apparent effects on yield. Researchers emphasize that multiple growing seasons will be needed to confirm these findings. The technology raises broader questions about the concentration of agricultural patents and the management of gene flow to wild relatives.
