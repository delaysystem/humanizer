Fecal microbiota transplantation, the transfer of gut microbes from healthy donors to patients, has emerged as a remarkably effective treatment for recurrent intestinal infections. For patients suffering from repeated Clostridioides difficile infections, which resist standard antibiotics and cause thousands of deaths annually, transplant procedures achieve cure rates approaching ninety percent. Regulatory agencies have now approved standardized commercial products, bringing consistency to a field that began with informal donor arrangements. Building on this success, investigators are exploring applications to a wider range of conditions linked to gut microbial imbalance. Trials in inflammatory bowel disease have shown meaningful but incomplete responses, with benefits often requiring repeated procedures.
