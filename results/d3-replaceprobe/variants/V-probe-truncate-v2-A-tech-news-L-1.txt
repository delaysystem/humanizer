A contentious debate over the accuracy of the state's official broadband coverage map continues to escalate, with hundreds of millions of dollars in federal funding hanging in the balance. The map, which is compiled from data self-reported by internet service providers, indicates that the vast majority of addresses have access to high-speed service. However, a coalition of rural counties disputes these figures, presenting tens of thousands of crowd-sourced speed tests that suggest coverage is significantly overstated. The discrepancy stems largely from the mapping methodology, under which an entire census block is classified as served if even a single location within it has access.
