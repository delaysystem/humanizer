Labor negotiations at one of the nation's busiest container ports have reached a critical juncture, with the current union contract set to expire within days. The central point of contention is the port authority's plan to expand automation to additional terminals. Management argues that automated cranes dramatically improve efficiency, citing significantly faster vessel turnaround times at the existing automated berth. Union leaders counter that automation has steadily eroded membership through attrition, even as cargo volumes reach new records. They question whether the productivity figures account for the full costs of system outages and maintenance. The stakes extend well beyond the waterfront.
