The annual influenza vaccine represents one of public health's persistent challenges, requiring scientists to predict months in advance which viral strains will circulate. Manufacturing constraints force strain selection decisions in February for the following winter, and mismatches substantially reduce effectiveness in some years. Messenger-RNA vaccine platforms offer potential solutions to these limitations. Because mRNA vaccines can be designed and manufactured far more quickly than traditional egg-based products, strain selection could occur closer to flu season when predictions are more reliable. Additionally, mRNA technology enables the inclusion of multiple strain components or conserved viral elements that change little between seasons.
