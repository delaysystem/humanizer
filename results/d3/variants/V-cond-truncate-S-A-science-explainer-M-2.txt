Earthquake early warning systems save lives by exploiting the different speeds of seismic waves. When an earthquake occurs, it generates fast-moving P-waves followed by slower but more destructive S-waves. Sensors near the epicenter detect the initial waves and automated systems rapidly estimate the earthquake's magnitude. Warnings are then transmitted to areas that the damaging waves have not yet reached, providing seconds to a minute of advance notice. This brief window allows for automated protective actions such as slowing trains and opening elevator doors. Japan operates the world's most advanced system, while California has deployed statewide coverage in recent years. The technology faces limitations, including occasional false alarms and underestimated magnitudes for the largest events. Engineers
