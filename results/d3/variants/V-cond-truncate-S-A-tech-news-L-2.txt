The massive semiconductor fabrication plant under construction outside Columbus faces a critical challenge that has little to do with technology: finding enough qualified workers. The facility is expected to employ thousands of technicians, engineers, and support staff when fully operational. However, the specialized skills required for chip manufacturing are in short supply throughout the region. Local community colleges have responded by rapidly developing new training programs, including clean-room laboratories and accelerated degree tracks. Enrollment has surpassed expectations, demonstrating strong interest among prospective students. Yet the programs face their own staffing difficulties, as experienced professionals can command far higher salaries in industry than in education.
