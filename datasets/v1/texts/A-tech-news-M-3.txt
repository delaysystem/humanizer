The Riverside school district is reconsidering its plan to purchase thousands of new laptops for students after bids exceeded the allocated budget. The district had intended to replace its aging fleet of devices with a standardized model featuring improved battery life and durability. However, the lowest qualifying bid came in substantially over budget due to rising component costs and new tariffs. School board members are now weighing several alternatives, including splitting the purchase across multiple vendors, postponing the refresh, or seeking additional funding through a ballot measure. Teachers have voiced strong support for proceeding without delay, citing the poor condition of current devices. The technology department has warned that managing multiple models would strain limited support resources. A final decision is expected at next month's board meeting. Parent organizations have mobilized in support of the refresh, arguing that outdated devices disadvantage students in an increasingly digital curriculum. The district's grant writer is pursuing federal connectivity funds that could offset a portion of the shortfall. Surrounding districts report similar procurement difficulties, suggesting systemic market pressures rather than local mismanagement. Device trade-in values for the oldest machines may recover a modest fraction of the needed funds.
