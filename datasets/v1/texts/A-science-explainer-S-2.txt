Vaccines protect against disease by training the immune system to recognize threats before real infection occurs. A vaccine contains a harmless form of a germ, such as a weakened virus or a fragment of it. When administered, the immune system responds by producing antibodies and forming memory cells. These memory cells persist for years, allowing for a rapid response if the actual pathogen is encountered. As a result, vaccinated individuals can fight off infections before symptoms develop. Booster doses help maintain this protection when immunity naturally decreases over time.
