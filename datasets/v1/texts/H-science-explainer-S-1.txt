Sunsets turn red for the same reason the daytime sky is blue: air scatters short wavelengths out of direct sunlight. At noon the path through the atmosphere is short, so mostly blue light gets scattered toward your eyes. At dusk the light travels a much longer slanting path, and by the time it arrives nearly all the blue has been scattered away, leaving reds and oranges. Dust, pollution, and volcanic ash amplify the effect by adding larger particles that scatter even the longer wavelengths sideways. That is why the most spectacular sunsets often follow storms or eruptions that wash or load the upper air.
