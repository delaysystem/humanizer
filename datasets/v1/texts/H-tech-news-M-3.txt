The Riverside school district's plan to replace 9,000 aging student laptops hit a snag this week when the winning bid came in $1.8 million over the technology budget. Administrators had hoped to standardize on a single rugged model with an eight-hour battery, but the lone qualifying vendor raised its price after a tariff hike on imported displays. The board now faces three options: split the order between two cheaper models, delay the refresh by a year, or ask voters for a supplemental levy in May. Teachers at Tuesday's meeting argued strongly against delay, noting that a fifth of the current fleet no longer holds a charge through first period. The district's technology director, Sam Osei, warned that mixing models would double the imaging workload for his four-person team. A decision is expected at the November board session. In the meantime, the district will extend warranties on the oldest machines, a stopgap Osei described as buying time at a premium.
