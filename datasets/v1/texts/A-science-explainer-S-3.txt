Ocean tides are primarily caused by the gravitational pull of the Moon on Earth's water. The Moon's gravity draws water toward it, creating a bulge on the facing side. A second bulge forms on the opposite side due to centrifugal effects from Earth's rotation. As the planet rotates through these bulges, most coastal areas experience two high tides and two low tides daily. The Sun's gravity also plays a role. When the Sun and Moon align, tides are especially pronounced, while perpendicular positions produce weaker tides.
