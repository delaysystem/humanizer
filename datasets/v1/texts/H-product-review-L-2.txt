Two hundred shots into the BaristaPro 9, I can tell you exactly who should buy it: someone who already weighs their coffee and wants the machine to stop being the excuse. Temperature stability is the headline — my thermometer logged 200°F plus or minus one degree across ten consecutive shots, a consistency my old single-boiler never approached. The rotary pump runs quiet enough to pull a shot without waking the house, and the three-hole steam tip textures milk in about thirty seconds once you learn its angle. The learning curve is real, though. The unpressurized baskets punish sloppy distribution, the manual pre-infusion rewards patience I did not have for the first month, and the drip tray fills faster than the 2.5-liter tank empties, which is a design choice I still do not understand. At $899 plus a capable grinder — budget another $300 minimum, since the built-in one on the bundle version is mediocre — this is a $1,200 commitment to the hobby. Café savings will not justify it quickly; at four dollars a latte, break-even lands around eighteen months of daily use. What justifies it is the cup. My flat whites now rival the café down the street, and on weekends they beat it. The grinder question nearly broke me. I started with a $150 conical burr unit and spent a month blaming the machine for sour shots before a forum post convinced me the grind was the culprit. Upgrading to a $380 flat-burr grinder changed everything overnight — suddenly the same beans, same dose, produced sweetness instead of battery acid. Lesson learned: budget the grinder first, then buy the machine you can still afford. Descaling every three months takes twenty minutes and the company sells the solution for $15, which feels steep for citric acid but keeps the warranty intact. The portafilter handles are real walnut and have aged beautifully. One genuine annoyance: the cup warmer on top is too narrow for two mugs, so my partner's latte waits its turn.
