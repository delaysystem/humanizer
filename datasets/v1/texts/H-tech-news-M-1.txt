Commuters who rely on the Metro Transit app woke up Monday to a complete overhaul two years in the making. The rewrite replaces a patchwork of five older tools with a single trip planner that covers buses, light rail, and the city's bike-share docks. Early reviews are cautiously positive: real-time arrival predictions now update every fifteen seconds instead of every two minutes, and a new disruption feed pushes alerts before riders reach the platform. Not everything survived the transition. Monthly-pass holders must re-verify their discount status, a process the agency admits takes up to three business days, and the popular widget for home-screen departures will not return until spring. Transit chief Maria Okafor said the agency held back several features to hit its October deadline after last year's failed launch attempt, which crashed within hours and took a week to restore. Privacy advocates won one concession: location history is now stored on the device rather than uploaded, reversing a policy that drew criticism in 2023. The agency says download numbers passed 200,000 by Wednesday afternoon, roughly double the old app's active user base.
