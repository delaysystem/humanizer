Eighteen months after a ransomware attack forced St. Anne's Hospital to divert ambulances for nine days, the facility's recovery has become a case study in what rebuilding actually costs. The attackers entered through a vendor's remote-access account in March of last year, encrypting patient records and knocking out the scheduling system. Rather than pay the demanded sum, the hospital's board authorized a full rebuild: new servers, segmented networks, and a policy requiring hardware keys for every staff login. The price tag reached $4.2 million, nearly triple the initial insurance estimate. Chief information officer David Lindqvist says the hardest part was not technical but human — retraining 1,400 employees who had shared passwords for years. Phishing-test failure rates fell from 31 percent to 6 percent after quarterly drills became mandatory. The hospital has since published its incident timeline and network diagrams, a rare act of transparency that security researchers have praised. Federal officials cited the case this month when proposing stricter backup requirements for critical-care facilities.
