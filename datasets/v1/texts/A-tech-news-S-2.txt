New legislation requiring smartphone manufacturers to make repair parts available to independent shops has gone into effect. The law aims to reduce electronic waste and lower repair costs for consumers. Under the new rules, manufacturers must provide screens, batteries, and other components at fair prices. Consumer rights groups have praised the measure as a victory for sustainability and affordability. Industry representatives, however, have raised concerns about quality control and intellectual property. Legal experts predict the law will face court challenges in the coming months. Similar measures are being considered in several other states, suggesting a broader national trend toward repairability.
