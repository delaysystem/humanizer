Sunsets appear red because of the way Earth's atmosphere scatters sunlight. When the sun is high in the sky, light travels a relatively short path through the air, and blue wavelengths scatter in all directions, making the sky look blue. Near sunset, sunlight must pass through much more atmosphere to reach your eyes. Along this longer journey, most blue light is scattered away, leaving predominantly red and orange wavelengths. Particles such as dust and pollution can intensify these colors. This is why sunsets are often most vivid after storms or volcanic activity.
