Ocean tides are mostly the Moon's doing, through gravity with a twist. The Moon pulls hardest on the water facing it, creating a bulge — that is one high tide. But inertia flings water outward on the far side too, making a second bulge opposite the first. As Earth rotates beneath these two bulges, most coastlines pass through two high tides and two low tides each day. The Sun adds a smaller pull: when Sun and Moon align at new and full moons, their forces combine into extra-high spring tides. When they sit at right angles, the tides partly cancel into weak neap tides.
