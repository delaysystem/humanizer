After six weeks with the RoboVac X9, I have stopped thinking of it as a gadget and started thinking of it as the member of the household that does the floors. It maps quickly — our 1,400-square-foot main level took one pass — and the room-by-room scheduling actually works: kitchen daily, bedrooms twice a week, office never during calls. Obstacle avoidance is the real upgrade over our old bump-and-spin model. It steered around shoes, cables, and, in one memorable test, a sleeping cat, though it did eat a sock in week two. The self-emptying dock holds about a month of debris and is genuinely quiet. Complaints are minor but real. It cannot climb our half-inch bathroom threshold, so that room stays manual. The app pushed two firmware updates that reset my no-go zones, which is the kind of bug that erodes trust. At $649 it is not cheap, but it replaced a $140 monthly cleaning visit for floors, so the math works in under five months.
