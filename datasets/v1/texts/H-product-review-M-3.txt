The ProMix 550 has kneaded roughly forty loaves in my kitchen since August, and the motor has not once sounded strained — the same cannot be said of the $180 mixer it replaced, which smelled of hot plastic by loaf six. The 5.5-quart bowl handles double batches of pizza dough without climbing the hook, and the metal gears, confirmed by the pry-happy teardown community, should outlast the ten-year warranty. Attachments are where the price stings: the pasta roller costs $129 extra and the grinder $99, turning a $449 mixer into a $700 system if you want the full lineup. The mixer head also has a slight wobble at high speed that the company calls normal and I call noticeable. Still, for anyone who bakes weekly, the core machine earns its keep. Weekend bread went from a project to a habit. The pasta attachment, bought on sale for $89, gets weekly use and paid for itself versus fresh-pasta prices within two months — flat sheets are foolproof, though the spaghetti cutter needs dough drier than the manual suggests. Cleanup is honest work: the bowl and hook go in the dishwasher, but flour dust reaches every corner of my kitchen regardless of the pouring shield. Nine months in, the hinge shows zero play and the paint has one chip from a dropped measuring cup. My neighbor's identical machine is twelve years old and still kneading, which is the review that matters most.
