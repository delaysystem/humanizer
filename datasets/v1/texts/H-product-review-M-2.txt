I read 23 books on the PaperGlow 3 this quarter, which tells you most of what matters. The 300-pixel-per-inch screen is as close to paper as e-ink gets, and the warm front light makes midnight reading easy on the eyes. Battery life is absurd in the best way — I charged it in September and again at Thanksgiving. The waterproofing survived a bathtub dunking that would have killed my old model. What keeps it from perfect: the store pushes sponsored titles aggressively, the page-turn buttons I loved on the previous generation are gone, and sideloading library books takes three more taps than it should. None of that outweighs the core experience. At $159, often discounted to $129, it is the reader I recommend to anyone who reads more than a book a month. The library integration deserves credit: borrowing through the public library app sends books to the device in under a minute, and I have not paid for a bestseller all year. Highlights sync to my phone's notes app, which finally killed my habit of photographing pages. The one software gripe that persists is search — finding a passage in a 900-page history book takes patience the hardware does not deserve. My daughter claimed the old model, and watching a ten-year-old navigate it without instructions says more about the interface than any spec sheet.
